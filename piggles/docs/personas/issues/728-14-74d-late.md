## What happened

Opened **Supplier performance** to see whether Ashcombe Mills had been keeping
to their dates. Their On time cell said:

> 50%
> 4 checked · **14.74d late**

## Why it matters

Two problems in six characters.

**`d` is a letter somebody has to be taught.** Twelve other places in this
console spell "day" and "days" out through the shared `plural` helper. Three
spots abbreviated, and one of them is on the screen a shop owner opens to decide
whether to keep buying from a business.

**14.74 is a precision nobody has.** The server measures an average and hands
back two decimal places. No owner is deciding anything differently at 14.74 days
than at 15; what the number buys with those two extra digits is the impression
that this is a laboratory reading rather than four deliveries.

## What was done

**`latenessLabel`** on the scorecard: whole days, in words. Under a day gets its
own sentence, because rounding 0.4 to "0 days late" would read as not late at
all.

| was         | now              |
| ----------- | ---------------- |
| 14.74d late | 15 days late     |
| 0.9d late   | under a day late |
| 1.2d late   | 1 day late       |

**`hoursLabel`** on the ship-direct figures, the other two: "6h" became "6
hours" and "1d 4h" became "1 day 4 hours".

Both consoles, because the rounding is not a brand question: an operator does
not need the hundredths either.
