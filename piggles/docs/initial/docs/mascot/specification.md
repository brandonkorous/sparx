# Piggles Mascot Specification & Creative Handoff

**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Canonical brand direction  
**Purpose:** Maintain a consistent Piggles character across product, marketing, animation, merchandise, social media, advertising, and future creative generation.

---

# 1. Brand Foundation

## Brand

**Piggles**

## Primary Domain

**getpiggles.com**

## Positioning

> **Business software for people who have a business to run.**

Piggles intentionally makes business software feel less corporate, less intimidating, and more human.

The brand should feel:

- Approachable
- Memorable
- Helpful
- Friendly
- Playful
- Competent
- Dependable
- Modern
- Slightly mischievous
- Never childish

Piggles is not simply a mascot placed beside the software.

**Piggles is the personality of the product.**

Users should naturally be able to think or say:

- “Ask Piggles.”
- “Piggles found something.”
- “Piggles has it handled.”
- “Let Piggles take care of that.”
- “Piggles says you're all caught up.”

---

# 2. Canonical Mascot

## Selected Base

**Round 2 — Option 1: Classic & Balanced**

This is the canonical Piggles character.

All future characters, poses, illustrations, animations, and role variants must originate from this base.

Do not independently redesign Piggles when creating a new scene.

---

# 3. Core Character Concept

Piggles is a very round, happy pig.

His proportions are intentionally exaggerated.

He should feel like a lovable little wrecking ball rather than a realistic pig.

The visual joke is simple:

**Piggles is extremely round and has ridiculously tiny limbs.**

That contrast is fundamental to the character.

---

# 4. Canonical Body Proportions

## Overall Shape

Piggles should have an almost circular overall silhouette.

Target visual proportions:

- Body/head combination forms approximately **70–80% of total visual mass**
- Width is approximately equal to or slightly greater than height
- Belly is pronounced and round
- Head should visually flow into the body rather than appearing as a separate large head
- Neck should essentially not be visible

Piggles should feel like:

**one giant round pig with little things attached to him.**

Not:

**a normal pig that happens to be overweight.**

---

# 5. Legs and Feet

Piggles has extremely small legs.

This is intentional.

## Legs

- Very short
- Barely visible beneath the body
- Positioned toward the outer lower edges
- Should never lengthen into normal cartoon-character legs

## Feet / Hooves

- Tiny
- Dark brown or similarly muted hoof color
- Rounded
- Slight outward positioning is acceptable
- Never oversized

The tiny feet reinforce the scale and roundness of the body.

---

# 6. Arms and Hands

Piggles has small arms relative to his body.

## Arms

- Short
- Rounded
- Soft
- Approximately proportional to those shown in the approved base mascot

## Hands

- Simplified cartoon hands
- Small rounded digits when needed
- Do not use realistic anatomy
- Should be expressive enough for pointing, holding objects, waving, thumbs-up, etc.

Accessories should scale appropriately to Piggles rather than forcing his anatomy to change.

---

# 7. Head and Face

Piggles' face is the emotional center of the character.

It must remain simple.

## Eyes

Default:

- Small
- Black
- Rounded/oval
- Friendly
- High contrast
- Minimal or no visible white

Avoid:

- Highly realistic eyes
- Large Disney-style eyes
- Anime eyes
- Excessive reflections
- Eyelashes as a default characteristic

The eyes should remain readable at very small sizes.

---

# 8. Snout

The snout is particularly important because it visually connects the character to the Piggles **P** logo.

## Shape

- Horizontally rounded
- Slightly protruding
- Soft pink slightly darker than surrounding skin
- Two vertically oriented oval nostrils

The nostrils should subtly echo the two vertical openings inside the Piggles logo.

This connection should remain consistent.

---

# 9. Ears

Piggles has:

- Two medium-small pig ears
- Rounded triangular form
- Slight outward angle
- Soft folds
- Slightly darker interior color

The ears should contribute to the silhouette without making Piggles look pointed or aggressive.

Avoid oversized ears.

---

# 10. Tail

Piggles has a tiny curly pig tail.

It should:

- Be visually subtle
- Appear only when the pose permits
- Be considerably smaller than typical cartoon pig tails
- Use a simple curl

The tail can become a small secondary animation opportunity.

It must not become a dominant design feature.

---

# 11. Skin

Piggles uses a warm, soft pig pink.

General direction:

- Light warm pink base
- Slightly darker snout
- Slight warmth/blush in cheeks
- Very soft highlights
- Subtle dimensional shading

Avoid:

- Neon pink body color
- Hyper-realistic skin
- Visible pores
- Heavy fur
- Plastic toy gloss
- Human-like skin rendering

Piggles may have a polished dimensional finish, but should remain clearly stylized.

---

# 12. Default Clothing

Canonical Piggles wears:

## Black T-Shirt

The shirt should be:

- Solid black or very dark charcoal
- Soft
- Rounded around the body
- Short sleeved
- Snug enough to reinforce the belly
- Simple

No default buttons, collars, pockets, stripes, trim, patterns, or decorative stitching.

---

# 13. Shirt Logo

The Piggles **P** symbol appears centered on the shirt.

## Rules

- Pink Piggles P
- Centered horizontally
- Positioned prominently on upper/middle torso
- Large enough to remain visible at small character sizes
- Maintain the actual Piggles P design
- Never substitute a generic pig icon
- Never redraw the logo as a different P

This mark is one of the strongest visual anchors tying the mascot to the product.

---

# 14. Canonical Default Expression

The canonical Piggles expression is:

**Open, happy smile.**

Characteristics:

- Friendly open mouth
- Simple happy eyes
- Relaxed eyebrows if visible
- Welcoming posture
- Positive energy

It should communicate:

> “Hey. I can help.”

Not:

> “LOOK HOW EXCITED I AM!”

Piggles is upbeat without constantly being hyperactive.

---

# 16. Personality

Piggles is:

## Helpful

He wants users to succeed.

## Capable

Piggles should never feel incompetent simply for comedy.

## Friendly

He removes intimidation from business software.

## Slightly Playful

Humor is welcome.

Clown behavior is not.

## Supportive

Piggles is a companion.

Not a lecturer.

## Curious

He may discover opportunities, issues, tasks, or insights.

## Confident

Piggles understands the software even when the user doesn't.

---

# 17. Personality Boundaries

Piggles should never be:

- Condescending
- Sarcastic toward the user
- Mean
- Judgmental
- Aggressive
- Exhaustingly cheerful
- Infantile
- Baby-talking
- Corporate
- Robotic
- Overly formal

Piggles may joke about business software.

Piggles should **not joke at the user's expense**.

---

# 18. Role Variant System

Role variants are **Piggles wearing or holding something**.

They are not separate characters.

Every role variant must begin from the canonical Piggles model.

The following must remain unchanged:

- Body proportions
- Face proportions
- Skin color
- Ear structure
- Snout
- Eyes
- Foot size
- Core silhouette
- Black shirt
- Pink P logo unless temporarily obscured by a functional accessory

---

# 19. Accessories

Accessories identify what Piggles is doing.

They should never redefine who Piggles is.

Good examples:

- Clipboard
- Laptop
- Headset
- Magnifying glass
- Shield
- Small tool
- Tablet
- Pencil
- Calculator
- Calendar
- Package
- Camera
- Megaphone

Accessories should be:

- Simple
- Easy to recognize
- Visually secondary
- Properly scaled
- Removable

If removing the accessory makes the character no longer look like Piggles, the design has drifted too far.

---

# 20. Clothing Variant Rule

Avoid changing Piggles' entire wardrobe for every role.

Preferred:

**Same black Piggles shirt + accessory**

Instead of:

- Construction costume
- Business suit
- Lab coat
- Superhero costume
- Police uniform
- Chef costume

Selective exceptions may be used for campaigns, but role variants should normally maintain the canonical shirt.

A hard hat for Builder Piggles is acceptable.

Replacing his entire outfit with construction clothing is not preferred.

# 23. Product Usage

Piggles may appear throughout the software.

## Empty States

Examples:

- Sleeping Piggles
- Waiting Piggles
- Piggles looking inside an empty box

Keep empty-state humor gentle.

---

## Loading States

Possible animation:

Piggles' tiny legs running while his body barely moves.

This contrast could become a recognizable recurring visual joke.

---

## Success States

Examples:

- Small jump
- Confetti
- Happy wave
- Thumbs up
- Tiny celebration

---

## Errors

Piggles should not appear terrified or injured.

Good:

- Confused Piggles
- Piggles inspecting a problem
- Slightly embarrassed Piggles

Avoid:

- Crying
- Panic
- Violence
- Faceplants as the default error representation

---

## Notifications

Piggles can:

- Point toward a notification
- Hold a small sign
- Peek into a UI element
- Pop up beside a card
- Carry a notification bubble

# 26. Visual Rendering Style

Current approved direction:

**Soft dimensional 3D character illustration.**

Characteristics:

- Smooth surfaces
- Gentle dimensional shading
- Rounded geometry
- Soft studio lighting
- Minimal texture
- Clean silhouette
- Modern animation-quality rendering

The character should feel like he could exist in a professionally animated commercial.

Avoid:

- Photorealism
- Hyperrealistic pig features
- Claymation unless intentionally designed as a campaign
- Flat clip-art style
- Anime
- Comic book rendering
- Generic corporate vector illustration
- Toy/plush appearance as the canonical rendering
- Excessive gloss

---

# 27. Silhouette Test

A successful Piggles illustration should still be recognizable when displayed:

- In silhouette
- At 64px
- Without accessories
- Without text
- Without scenery

The key recognition features are:

- Huge round body
- Tiny legs
- Small pig ears
- Small arms
- Pig snout
- Black shirt
- Friendly stance

---

# 28. Logo Relationship

The mascot and logo should feel related without literally sharing the same shape.

The mascot's:

- Snout nostrils
- Roundness
- Pink palette
- Shirt mark

visually connect Piggles to the Piggles P.

Do not attempt to reshape the mascot body into the letter P.

The logo is the symbol.

Piggles is the character.

---

# 29. Character Scale

Piggles should usually be visually compact.

Avoid making him tall or lanky.

When placed beside humans or environmental objects, Piggles may be treated as an intentionally stylized mascot without requiring a fixed real-world physical height.

Consistency of **proportion** matters more than realistic scale.

---

# 30. Animation Principles

Piggles' body provides an opportunity for distinctive motion.

## Movement

Because his body is so round and his limbs are tiny:

- Walk cycles may have subtle waddling
- Fast running can exaggerate tiny moving feet
- Stops may have slight body momentum
- Jumps should feel heavy but soft
- Body may subtly squash and rebound

Avoid extreme rubber-hose deformation.

---

## Tail

The tail may:

- Bounce
- Wiggle
- Curl tighter
- React subtly to emotion

---

## Ears

Ears may provide secondary animation:

- Small bounce
- Slight lift
- Gentle reaction

Keep subtle.

---

# 31. Voice and Copy Relationship

Piggles' visual personality should match the written brand voice.

Good:

> “You're all caught up.”

> “Two customers are waiting for you.”

> “Nice. Another sale.”

> “Piggles found something.”

> “Your week is filling up.”

Less appropriate:

> “Congratulations! You have successfully completed the customer relationship management workflow.”

Piggles reduces the language of business software to normal human language.

---

# 32. Creative North Star

Whenever deciding whether something fits the mascot, use this question:

> **Would this make someone who hates business software feel more comfortable using business software?**

If yes, it probably fits Piggles.

If it makes the character feel:

- complicated
- corporate
- childish
- gimmicky
- chaotic

reconsider it.

---

# 33. Do

- Keep Piggles very round
- Keep his limbs tiny
- Keep the face simple
- Maintain black shirt + pink P
- Give him expressive poses
- Use recognizable props
- Let Piggles have fun
- Preserve consistency across variants
- Design for animation
- Design for small-scale recognition
- Use humor selectively

---

# 34. Don't

- Make Piggles skinny
- Make Piggles tall
- Give him realistic pig anatomy
- Give him human proportions
- Change his facial structure between scenes
- Randomly change shirt style
- Redesign the P logo
- Add unnecessary accessories
- Turn every role into a costume
- Make Piggles look like a children's educational character
- Make him overly glossy like a plastic toy
- Give him dramatically different personalities for different modules
- Treat variants as separate pigs

---

# 35. Image Generation Master Guidance

When generating new Piggles assets, every prompt should establish the canonical character before describing the scene.

Use the following conceptual structure:

**Character first → pose → expression → accessory → environment.**

Example:

> Canonical Piggles mascot: extremely round warm-pink pig, tiny legs and small rounded arms, medium-small pig ears, simple black oval eyes, centered rounded pink snout with two vertical nostrils, friendly open smile, wearing a plain black short-sleeve shirt with the official pink Piggles P centered on the chest. Preserve identical proportions and facial structure to the approved Piggles reference mascot.

Then add:

> Piggles is holding a clipboard and pencil, representing planning and project organization.

Do not begin prompts with:

> “Create a pig project manager…”

That invites visual drift.

Always begin with:

> **“Canonical Piggles mascot…”**

---

# 36. Reference Hierarchy

When visual references conflict, use this priority:

**1. Canonical Round 2 Option 1 mascot**  
**2. Original Piggles brand/logo board**  
**3. Approved role-character discovery board**  
**4. Other generated Piggles assets**

The canonical mascot always wins.

# 38. Final Canonical Statement

**Piggles is one character.**

He may:

- build
- organize
- communicate
- protect
- celebrate
- discover
- explain
- help

But he is always the same Piggles.

The goal of this system is for a customer to encounter Piggles anywhere — an advertisement, product screen, hat, sticker, video, email, or social post — and immediately recognize him before seeing the wordmark.

That consistency is the foundation of the Piggles character brand.
