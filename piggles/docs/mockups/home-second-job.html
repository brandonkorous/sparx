Here is the job description nobody gave you.

Six roles, with the hours you actually work them. Read the last line of each one first — it is the only part that matters, and it is the same shape every time.

  • Bookkeeper

    Sunday, after dinner

    Takings from the till, takings from the website, receipts out of a carrier bag, and a spreadsheet that has to agree with all three by the end of the evening.

    Why this job existsBecause the money arrives in four places and is recorded in a fifth.

  • Receptionist

    Every time the phone goes

    "Can you do Saturday?" You check a calendar, a notebook and your memory, and you give an answer that might be wrong until you get back to the desk.

    Why this job existsBecause the diary the public books into isn't the diary you're looking at.

  • Web person

    Whenever something changes

    New price, new opening hours, that thing you no longer sell. Every change happens twice — once in real life, and once on a website that has no idea.

    Why this job existsBecause your website is a separate company that happens to have your name on it.

  • Stock controller

    Tuesday, and again Friday

    Counting things you have already counted, because the number on the screen stopped being true at some point and nobody can say when.

    Why this job existsBecause two systems count the same shelf and neither tells the other.

  • IT department

    Always at the worst moment

    The card reader won't talk to the till. The booking confirmations stopped sending. Nobody is coming to fix it, so you learn just enough to make it limp along.

    Why this job existsBecause you own six products from six companies, and the joins are yours.

  • Software buyer

    Right now, probably

    Comparison tabs, free trials you forgot to cancel, and a nagging sense that there is a right answer and you have not found it. This one is not even a job. It is homework.

    Why this job existsBecause fixing one gap means buying one more thing that doesn't know about the others.

Five of those six are not jobs at all.

Nothing on that list needs a person. Every one of them is you carrying a fact from one piece of software to another, by hand, because the two of them have never been introduced. That is not admin. That is manual labour on behalf of your suppliers.

You are the only thing in your business that knows everything.

And it works, right up until you are busy, or ill, or on holiday, or trying to let somebody else answer the phone. The thing standing between you and a day off is not the work — it is that all of it lives in your head, because there is nowhere else for it to live.
