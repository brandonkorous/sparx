## 1. The one-sentence position

> **WizeWorks builds the software a business actually runs on — and because we already own the
> platform underneath it, we start most of the way finished.**

Everything else in this folder is downstream of that sentence. If a page, a headline, or a
screenshot doesn't serve it, it doesn't ship.

**Decided 2026-07-30:** WizeWorks leads as a **solution provider that also has products.** The
spine of the site is _how we solve business problems_; the products are the evidence and the
leverage — not the opening pitch. See [01-design-research-2026.md](01-design-research-2026.md) §15.

## 2. The wedge

There are two normal ways to get business software, and both of them are bad deals. WizeWorks
exists in the gap between them.

| Path                  | What you get                                               | What it costs you                                                                                          |
| --------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Hire an agency**    | Something built for you                                    | Everything is built from zero, on your money and your clock. You pay to invent things that already exist.  |
| **Buy off-the-shelf** | Something you can start using this week                    | It almost fits. You reshape the business around the software, and pay monthly for the parts you never use. |
| **WizeWorks**         | Something built for you, on a platform that already exists | The 80% that every business needs is already built and running. We build the 20% that is actually yours.   |

**The wedge, stated plainly for use in copy:**

> Most software companies sell you a product and hope it fits. Most agencies build from scratch and
> bill you for it. We own the platform, so we can hand you something that fits exactly — without
> starting from nothing.

This is a claim we can actually back, because sparx exists, is deployed, and runs real businesses.
That is the difference between a positioning statement and a slogan.

## 3. What WizeWorks is

- A **software studio** in Visalia, California, founded by Brandon Korous.
- A **platform owner** — sparx, our content and commerce operating system, is ours end to end.
- A **product house** — kanNINJA (project management) and AGCONN (connecting Central Valley
  farmworkers to verified seasonal jobs and training) are ours too.
- An **operator** — we host, monitor, and stay on call for what we build. It doesn't get handed
  over and forgotten.
- **Small and honest about it.** Small is a feature here: you talk to the person building it.

## 4. What WizeWorks is not

Say these out loud on the site. Explicit differentiation is one of the strongest signals both to
a buying committee and to an answer engine ([research §10](01-design-research-2026.md)).

- **Not a marketing agency.** We don't run your ads or manage your social calendar. We build the
  software those things point at.
- **Not a staffing firm.** You're not renting developers by the hour and managing them yourself.
- **Not a template shop.** Nothing here is a theme with your logo dropped in.
- **Not an enterprise-only vendor.** A two-person studio is a real customer, not a lead we
  disqualify.
- **Not industry-specific.** We have no vertical. That's deliberate — see §6.
- **Not an AI company.** We use AI where it earns its place, on your credentials, and we say so.
  We do not sell intelligence as a feature.

## 5. Who this is for

### The primary reader

**A business owner or operator who is not technical.** They run the company. They know exactly
what is broken about how the work flows today, and they have no vocabulary for the software that
would fix it — nor should they need one.

They are:

- Somewhere between 2 and 400 employees.
- Currently running the business on a stack of five things that don't talk to each other, plus a
  spreadsheet that one person maintains and everyone depends on.
- Burned once already, by an agency that disappeared or a platform that didn't fit.
- Evaluating this on a phone, between other things, with about 40 seconds of patience.

**Everything on the site is written for this person.** Zero assumed technical vocabulary. Any term
that can't be avoided gets defined in the same sentence.

### The rest of the committee

A real decision involves more people than the reader above. The page must serve them without
telling anyone a different story — one narrative, layered by depth
([research §5](01-design-research-2026.md)).

| Who                     | What they need to find                                    | Where it lives                                        |
| ----------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| **Owner / decision**    | Does this solve my problem, and can I afford it?          | Hero, outcomes, pricing band, the software itself     |
| **Operator / daily**    | Will this make my day better or worse?                    | How-we-work section, real screenshots, industry pages |
| **Technical evaluator** | Is this real software or a wrapper? Who owns the data?    | Architecture page, security page, docs links          |
| **Finance**             | What does it cost, forever, and what happens if we leave? | Pricing, ownership/exit statement, hosting page       |

### Who this is not for

Saying no is part of the position:

- Companies looking for the cheapest possible bid.
- Companies that want a logo, a brochure site, and nothing behind it.
- Companies that want to hand over a spec and never speak to us again.

## 6. "Every company, big or small, tech or beauty"

This is the defining constraint, and it is also the hardest claim to make credible. A general
claim is empty on its own; it becomes true by demonstration
([research §11](01-design-research-2026.md)).

**The rule: generality is asserted once, and proven eight times.**

One line on the homepage says we work with any business. Then eight industry pages each speak
that industry's actual language, use that industry's actual words for its actual problems, and
show a real artifact from that world.

### The industry set

Chosen for maximum spread — size, sector, technical literacy, and whether they sell things,
time, or expertise. Each carries its own hue — the engine that keeps the site from going monotone
([brand §4.2](04-brand-and-visual-identity.md)).

| Industry                                 | What their day is                                              | The artifact we show                          | Hue                  |
| ---------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | -------------------- |
| **Retail & specialty shops**             | Inventory, walk-ins, online orders, one person doing all three | A live product page + stock across locations  | Tobacco `#8A5A2B`    |
| **Beauty, wellness & personal care**     | Bookings, no-shows, clients, retail on the side                | A booking calendar + a client record          | Plum `#6E3F55`       |
| **Trades & field service**               | Estimates, dispatch, invoices chased by phone                  | An estimate → invoice → paid trail            | Brick `#9C4A2F`      |
| **Professional services**                | Proposals, retainers, deliverables, billable time              | A pipeline + a recurring invoice              | Slate teal `#2F5C64` |
| **Manufacturing & distribution**         | Dealer pricing, POs, terms, fulfillment                        | A wholesale price list + an approval queue    | Iron `#4A5560`       |
| **Food & hospitality**                   | Menus, catering orders, locations, hours that change           | A menu that updates once and lands everywhere | Olive `#63702F`      |
| **Health & clinical practices**          | Scheduling, intake, follow-up, records that must not leak      | An intake form + a consent trail              | Sage `#4C7D6E`       |
| **Nonprofits, associations & education** | Members, donations, events, publishing on a shoestring         | A member directory + an event page            | Aubergine `#513B63`  |

### The anti-rule

**No industry may become the default lens.** If an example, screenshot, headline, or piece of
sample data assumes auto parts, diesel, or any single vertical, it is a bug. Vary the vertical
deliberately and constantly, including in screenshots — the demo data on the site should show a
salon on one page and a distributor on the next.

## 7. The offer ladder

Four ways to work with us, in increasing order of involvement. The site should make it obvious
which rung a visitor is on without making them read all four.

| Rung             | What it is                                                         | Who it's for                                                  |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. Use it**    | Sign up for sparx, kanNINJA, or AGCONN and run it yourself         | Small teams who want to move today                            |
| **2. Set it up** | We configure the platform to your business and hand you the keys   | Businesses who know what they need but not how to build it    |
| **3. Build it**  | Custom modules, integrations, and workflows on top of the platform | Businesses whose operation doesn't fit anything off the shelf |
| **4. Run it**    | We host, monitor, and stay on call — indefinitely                  | Businesses with no IT function and no desire for one          |

**Copy implication:** rungs 2–4 are the solution-provider business and belong on the spine of the
homepage. Rung 1 is the product business and belongs in the portfolio section and the product
sites. Do not lead with rung 1 — that would make WizeWorks look like a SaaS vendor with a
consulting arm, which inverts the decided position.

## 8. Proof strategy — the work is the proof

**Decided 2026-07-30: this site carries no social proof.** No testimonials, no logo wall, no
customer stories, no counts. Not "none yet, pending clearance" — none, as a position.

That is a deliberate departure from the 2026 consensus, which treats distributed social proof as
the central credibility mechanism ([research §9](01-design-research-2026.md)). The consensus is
describing companies whose product cannot be shown. Ours can.

**What we show instead — proof by artifact:**

1. **The software itself, at real fidelity.** Real screens doing real work. A visitor who can see
   the thing operating does not need a stranger's opinion that it operates.
2. **The scope of what exists**, stated in specifics rather than adjectives — the modules, the
   platform surface, what it runs today.
3. **The wedge, demonstrated.** "We own the platform" is checkable: sparx is public, live, and
   sellable. That claim proves itself.
4. **Candor about stage.** Small studio, early company, stated plainly.
5. **Concrete process.** [Offer ladder §7](#7-the-offer-ladder) with real steps and real
   durations. What it's like to work with us, described precisely, is a credibility instrument.

**Never on this site:**

- Testimonials, named or anonymous.
- A logo wall.
- Customer counts, team size, or years in business we don't have.
- Case studies presented as proof of demand.
- Numbers inside images — an answer engine can't read them.

**Design consequence:** the homepage has no "proof" section and the sitemap has no `/work`
route. The portfolio and the industry pages carry the entire burden, which means their screenshots
must be genuinely good. That raises the bar on the artifacts rather than lowering it on the site.

## 9. Messaging hierarchy

What we say, in priority order. Level 1 must survive alone; each level below adds depth for
someone who kept reading.

**Level 1 — the claim (must be repeatable after 5 seconds)**

> We build the software your business runs on.

**Level 2 — the wedge (why us and not the two normal options)**

> Most companies sell you a product and hope it fits, or build from scratch and bill you for it.
> We own the platform underneath, so we can build exactly what your business needs — starting from
> most of the way done.

**Level 3 — the proof (why that's believable)**

> sparx is ours: a full content, commerce, and operations platform, deployed and running real
> businesses. kanNINJA is ours too. When we build for you, we're not learning on
> your money.

**Level 4 — the range (why "every company" is true)**

> A two-chair salon and a 40-truck distributor need different software and the same things
> underneath: a site people find, a way to get paid, records that stay straight, and one place to
> see it all. We've built that. We shape it to you.

**Level 5 — the terms (what it's actually like)**

> You talk to the person building it. We host it and stay on call. Your data is yours and leaves
> with you if you go.

## 10. Words we use and words we don't

| Don't say                   | Say                                               | Why                                            |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| solutions (as a noun)       | the software, the system, what we build           | "Solutions" is the emptiest word in B2B        |
| leverage, synergy, seamless | plain verbs                                       | Consultant filler; means nothing to an owner   |
| digital transformation      | replacing the spreadsheet, getting off paper      | Nobody outside a conference says this          |
| enterprise-grade            | it doesn't fall over — and here's the uptime      | Adjective pretending to be evidence            |
| cutting-edge, next-gen      | (nothing — show the thing)                        | Every slop page says it                        |
| stack, deploy, API-first    | (define inline, or don't use it)                  | Reader is not technical                        |
| merchant                    | business, company, owner — unless they truly sell | Not everyone sells things                      |
| unlock, empower, elevate    | a verb describing what actually happens           | Outcome poetry; unquotable by an answer engine |

Full treatment in [03-voice-and-tone.md](03-voice-and-tone.md).
