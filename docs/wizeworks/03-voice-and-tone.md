# WizeWorks — Voice & Tone

**Version:** 1.0.0

## 1. The voice in one line

> **A capable person explaining, plainly, what they will build for you and what it will cost —
> to someone who runs a business and does not care how software works.**

Not a brand. Not a brochure. A competent human being talking straight.

---

## 2. The five principles

### 2.1 Plain before clever

The reader is a business owner with 40 seconds. Every sentence trades against their patience.
Write the sentence a smart person would say out loud, then delete the part that was for us.

> ❌ WizeWorks delivers bespoke, enterprise-grade digital solutions engineered to accelerate
> operational excellence.
>
> ✅ We build the software your business runs on.

### 2.2 Specific beats impressive

A number, a name, a date, or a duration outperforms any adjective. If a claim can't be made
specific, it probably isn't a claim.

> ❌ Dramatically faster quoting.
>
> ✅ Quotes went from three days to forty minutes.

### 2.3 Say the hard part

Small studio. Early company. Real limits. Stating them plainly buys more trust than hiding them
costs. Readers are extremely well-calibrated at detecting inflation, and one detected exaggeration
discredits every other sentence on the page.

> ✅ We're small. You'll talk to the person building your software, because that's who's here.

### 2.4 No assumed vocabulary

The reader does not know what an API, a deploy, a stack, or a webhook is, and does not need to.
If a technical term is genuinely unavoidable, define it in the same breath and move on.

> ❌ Fully headless, API-first architecture.
>
> ✅ Your website, your orders, and your customer records all read from the same place — so a price
> you change once is right everywhere.

### 2.5 Never sell what we don't have

No claimed customer counts. No implied team size. No "trusted by thousands." No borrowed
credibility — and no testimonials at all. Every proof point on the site must be a thing we can
show, not a thing someone said about us ([02 §8](02-positioning-and-audience.md)).

## 3. Register

**Second person, present tense, active voice.** "You" is the business owner. "We" is WizeWorks.
Never "our clients" in a sentence aimed at a client.

| Dial         | Setting                                                                                           |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Formality    | Low-mid. Contractions yes. Slang no. Would read fine in an email to a customer.                   |
| Confidence   | High, evidence-backed. Confident about what we've built; honest about what we haven't.            |
| Warmth       | Present but not performed. No exclamation points. No emoji in body copy.                          |
| Humor        | Dry, rare, never at the reader's expense, never in a headline.                                    |
| Technicality | Zero by default. Full depth available on the architecture and security pages, clearly signposted. |
| Urgency      | None manufactured. No countdowns, no "limited spots," no false scarcity.                          |

---

## 4. Sentence mechanics

- **Sentences average under 20 words.** Vary the length — a short one after two long ones is how
  writing gets a pulse.
- **Lead with the subject and the verb.** Front-load the point; the qualification comes after.
- **One idea per paragraph.** Every paragraph must survive being quoted alone — this is both good
  writing and how an answer engine reads the page ([research §10](01-design-research-2026.md)).
- **Numerals for numbers**, including under ten, when they're data. Spell them out in prose.
- **Serial comma. Em dashes with no surrounding spaces — like this. Sentence case everywhere,
  including headings and buttons.**
- **No exclamation points** anywhere on the site.
- **No rhetorical questions as headings.** A real question the reader asks is fine and useful; a
  fake one ("Ready to grow?") is not.

## 5. Headline patterns that work

Six to ten words. Outcome first. A verb the reader would use.

| Pattern              | Shape                                  | Example                                              |
| -------------------- | -------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| **The plain claim**  | We [verb] the [thing] you [outcome]    | We build the software your business runs on          |
| **The named pain**   | [Familiar broken thing], [fixed]       | Five systems that don't talk to each other, replaced |
| **The number**       | [Metric] went from [before] to [after] | Quotes went from three days to forty minutes         |
| **The refusal**      | No [common bad thing]                  | No rebuild. No rip-out. No twelve-month project.     |
| **The direct offer** | [Verb] [thing], [qualifier]            | Tell us what's broken. We'll tell you what it takes. |

**Never:**

- A question that isn't real — "Ready to transform your business?"
- An abstract noun as the subject — "Innovation, delivered."
- A benefit with no object — "Move faster." Faster at what?
- Anything that would also fit a competitor's page unchanged.

## 6. Tone by surface

The voice never changes. The tone shifts with what the reader is doing.

| Surface                   | Tone                        | Notes                                                                      |
| ------------------------- | --------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Homepage hero**         | Direct, plain, confident    | One claim, repeatable in 5 seconds. Nothing clever.                        |
| **How we work**           | Concrete, procedural        | Real steps with real durations. This is where trust is actually built.     |
| **Industry pages**        | Fluent in their language    | Use their words for their problems. Never explain their business to them.  |
| **Product portfolio**     | Factual, one sentence each  | What it is, who it's for, link out. Resist selling here.                   |
| **Pricing**               | Blunt to the point of stark | Real numbers or a real range and an honest reason. Zero hedging.           |
| **Architecture/security** | Technical, precise          | The one place jargon is allowed, because a technical evaluator is reading. |
| **Contact / forms**       | Short, low-friction, human  | Say what happens next and how long it takes.                               |
| **Legal**                 | Plain-language first        | Readable summary above the binding text.                                   |
| **Errors & empty states** | Helpful, never cute         | What happened, what to do, who to reach. No mascots apologizing.           |
| **Email**                 | Like a person wrote it      | No template voice. Signed by a human.                                      |

## 7. The rewrite table

Reference for anyone writing a line for this site. Left column is what shows up in a first draft.

| Instead of                               | Write                                                         |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Bespoke solutions tailored to your needs | We build it to fit how you already work                       |
| Leverage our expertise                   | We've built this before                                       |
| Seamless integration                     | It connects to [the actual thing], and here's what that means |
| Enterprise-grade reliability             | It's been up 99.9% this year                                  |
| Empower your team                        | Your team stops re-typing the same order into two systems     |
| Cutting-edge technology                  | (delete — show the software instead)                          |
| Digital transformation                   | Getting off the spreadsheet                                   |
| Unlock new opportunities                 | (delete — name the opportunity)                               |
| Streamline your operations               | One place instead of five                                     |
| We're passionate about...                | (delete — nobody buys on your feelings)                       |
| Trusted by businesses everywhere         | (delete — show the software instead)                          |
| Best-in-class                            | (delete)                                                      |
| Get started today!                       | Tell us what's broken                                         |
| Learn more                               | See how it works / Read the story / See pricing               |
| Contact us                               | Book a 20-minute call / Send us the problem                   |
| Robust, scalable, powerful               | (delete all three — they modify nothing)                      |

**The universal test:** if the sentence would fit unchanged on a competitor's site, it isn't
saying anything. Rewrite it until it could only be ours.

## 8. Calls to action

Buttons say what happens, not what the reader should feel.

| Context                | Primary                        | Secondary            |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------ | -------------------- |
| Homepage hero          | Tell us what's broken          | See what we've built |
| Industry page          | Book a 20-minute call          | See how it works     |
| Product card           | Visit [product]                | —                    |
| Pricing                | Start a project                | Ask about hosting    |
| Contact form submitted | (confirmation copy, no button) | —                    |

**Rules:** sentence case, never all-caps. One primary action per screen. The low-friction option
is always available and always second. Never gate the first meaningful thing behind an email.

## 9. Worked example — the homepage hero

**Draft 1 (what a first pass produces):**

> ### Empowering businesses through innovative digital solutions
>
> WizeWorks partners with forward-thinking organizations to deliver cutting-edge, enterprise-grade
> software solutions tailored to their unique needs.
>
> `[Get Started]` `[Learn More]`

Every failure at once: abstract subject, no claim, five empty adjectives, no reader in the
sentence, and two buttons that describe nothing.

**Draft 2 (voice applied):**

> ### We build the software your business runs on
>
> Most companies sell you a product and hope it fits. Most agencies build from scratch and bill you
> for it. We own the platform underneath — so we build exactly what your business needs, starting
> from most of the way done.
>
> `[Tell us what's broken]` `[See what we've built]`

Twelve words of claim, three sentences that place us against the two real alternatives, and two
actions that say what happens.

## 10. Worked example — an industry page opening

**Beauty, wellness & personal care:**

> ### Your calendar is the business. Everything else should follow it.
>
> Bookings live in one app, client notes live in another, and the retail you sell at the front desk
> lives in a third — so nobody knows what a regular is actually worth. We put the calendar, the
> client record, the products, and the website on the same system. Book a client on Tuesday and
> the reminder, the notes, the receipt, and the rebooking prompt all already know who they are.

Note what it does: names their day in their words, names the specific broken thing, and describes
one concrete Tuesday rather than a benefit. No adjectives doing load-bearing work.

## 11. Writing for answer engines

A meaningful share of first contact is a machine reading the site to summarize it for a buyer.
This costs us nothing, because everything it wants is also good writing.

- **Say the entity plainly, early.** "WizeWorks is a software studio in Visalia, California that
  builds and operates business software." Stated, not implied.
- **Answer real questions under real question headings.** "What does it cost to work with
  WizeWorks?" then answer it in the next paragraph.
- **Self-contained paragraphs.** Each one should survive being quoted with no context.
- **Numbers in text**, never only inside an image.
- **Explicit comparisons.** What we are, what we are not, who we are not for. Answer engines
  reward and reproduce clear differentiation.
- **Maintain `llms.txt`** with the entity statement, the products, the offer ladder, and pricing.

## 12. Accessibility of language

Part of the voice, not a separate compliance task.

- **Aim at a 9th-grade reading level** for all customer-facing copy. Check it.
- **Link text describes its destination.** Never "click here," never a bare URL in prose.
- **Alt text describes the content**, not the file. For a decorative image, empty alt.
- **No meaning carried by color alone.** A status that's red also says what it is.
- **Expand every abbreviation on first use**, per page.
- **Use they/them** for any person whose pronouns aren't known — customers, readers, examples.

---

## 13. Hard bans

Non-negotiable, on every WizeWorks surface:

1. Exclamation points.
2. The word "solutions" as a standalone noun.
3. Testimonials of any kind — this site carries no social proof ([02 §8](02-positioning-and-audience.md)).
4. Claimed customers, counts, or scale we don't have.
5. Manufactured urgency of any kind.
6. Uppercase micro-labels above headings — this is the copy half of the no-eyebrows rule
   ([CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md) RULE #2).
7. "Click here" as link text.
8. Technical jargon in customer-facing copy without an inline definition.
9. Auto parts, diesel, or any single industry as the default example.
10. Any sentence that would fit unchanged on someone else's website.
